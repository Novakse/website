Where you skate

A fifteen-kilometre track, swept across a frozen lake. Beyond that, there's no one.

Every winter a long track is swept on Lake Orsa in Dalarna. You skate in a small group, with Joey out front reading the ice and deciding where the day will be at its best.

What you'll actually skate is never fixed in advance. Natural ice can't be planned: every morning we look again at the weather and ice quality, and adjust the route, the lake or the whole programme accordingly.

The group stays small and availability is limited — that's exactly why it works.

Where you sleep

Your own cabin, right by the ice

You stay in cabins at First Camp Orsa-Dalarna. No hotel corridor, but your own place where you gather in the evening and step straight onto the ice in the morning.

The exact layout of the cabins is arranged to match the group size. Just ask when you submit your request.

How it works

You register in pairs, we arrange the rest.

Orsa is an open-registration trip: you book a spot on a fixed departure date and skate together with the other participants. Sign-up is in pairs. If you come with your own group, you choose the date yourselves and we put the programme together with you.

4 to 12 participants

The trip runs from 4 participants and takes a maximum of 12.

Deposit at booking

Half at booking, the remainder no later than 5 weeks before departure.

Joey comes along

Years of experience on natural ice, and the one who judges the ice.

Day by day

What a week looks like

The order is fixed, the details aren't. We decide where we skate each morning, based on the weather and the ice.

Day 1

Arrival

You arrive at the cabins by Lake Orsa. Time to get your gear ready, get to know each other and take a first look at the ice. Joey talks the group through what the coming days may bring.

Day 2

The first kilometres

A relaxed start on the swept track, so everyone gets used to the natural ice and the skates. Joey watches how the group is doing and adjusts the pace accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions about Orsa

Is the skating trip to Orsa guided?

Yes, the skating trip to Orsa is a guided group trip with open enrolment. You book a spot on a fixed travel date and skate together with the other participants, with Joey out in front reading the ice. If you come with your own group, you choose the date yourselves and we put the programme together with you.

How long is the skating track on Lake Orsa?

On Lake Orsa in Dalarna a long track of about 15 kilometres is swept every winter. What you actually skate is never fixed in advance: every morning we look again at the weather and the ice quality, and adjust the route or the lake accordingly.

Where do you stay during the trip to Orsa?

You stay in your own cabins at First Camp Orsa-Dalarna, right by the ice. No hotel corridor, but a place of your own where you sit together in the evening and step straight onto the ice in the morning. We match the exact cabin arrangement to the size of the group.

What is and isn't included in the trip to Orsa?

Included are the accommodation in the cabins, local transport and guidance on the ice by Joey. Not included are travel to and from Sweden, your own skating equipment and winter clothing, mandatory safety gear (ice picks and a helmet) and insurance.

How good a skater do I need to be?

You don't have to be a competitive skater, but you do need to be able to skate independently and in control. We work with three paces: recreational, sporty and fanatic. If you're unsure about your level, Joey will think it through with you beforehand.

How much do we skate per day?

That depends on the pace you choose: recreational is 1–4 hours a day, sporty 2–5 hours and fanatic 3–6 hours. How many kilometres that adds up to is never fixed in advance — that is determined by the ice, the weather and the route of that day. See the three paces on the travel information page.

Is Orsa suitable for my first skating trip to Sweden?

Orsa is a guided group trip, and it is exactly that guidance which helps if you still have little experience with Scandinavian natural ice. Joey comes along, reads the ice and decides each morning where the day will come into its own best. So you don't have to judge for yourself where and when you can skate. Still, it remains natural ice: good preparation and following instructions are part of it.

What happens if the ice in Orsa isn't good enough?

Natural ice can't be guaranteed. That's why on this guided trip we look again every morning at the weather and the ice quality, and adjust the route, the lake or the programme accordingly. Where necessary we move to another suitable lake or choose an alternative programme.

Which skates do I need?

You bring your own skating equipment. For natural ice we recommend touring skates. You also need winter clothing you can spend a whole day outdoors in; ice picks and a helmet are mandatory.

Can I come along if someone else in my travel party skates faster or slower?

Give your level when you send in your request. Joey will then look at how this fits in best within the group.
